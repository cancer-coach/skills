# Requesting Records and Tissue Safely

Sources checked: **2026-10-15**. Procedures and legal rights depend on jurisdiction, institution, material, and circumstances. Help draft requests; do not assert automatic ownership, unconditional release, fixed legal deadlines, or guaranteed access to raw data.

## Request the material needed for a decision

Ask the treating team or records office which reports and images are needed. For a second opinion, ask the receiving institution about secure transfer, formats, pathology review requirements and authorization. [NCI: Finding Cancer Care](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/managing-care/finding-cancer-care).

For tissue, contact pathology with the receiving laboratory's instructions. Ask what exists, what can be released or loaned, what must remain for diagnosis and future care, and who coordinates shipment. Do not divert tissue needed for clinical pathology or request an additional invasive procedure solely because an AI checklist suggests it. Discuss optional banking before a planned procedure if useful, without delaying necessary care.

## Draft request: review before sending

```text
To: [verified records office or pathology department]
Purpose: [second opinion / ongoing care / personal copy]

Please explain your process for requesting:
[relevant reports, imaging files, slides, or other specific material]
From: [procedure or care episode, with identifiers added only through
       the institution's approved secure process]

Receiving clinician/institution, if applicable: [verified destination]
Please confirm authorization requirements, availability, format,
fees, expected timing, and any retention or return requirements.

Preferred secure contact method: [completed privately]
```

If access is unclear or refused, ask for the reason and the relevant institutional process; a patient navigator, records/privacy office, or qualified local adviser can help. Do not recommend deception or bypassing safeguards.

## Protect records and genomic information

- Use minimum necessary excerpts; remove names, birth dates, record numbers, contact details and unnecessary dates before AI use where feasible.
- Check documents, image metadata and filenames; removing a visible name alone is not reliable deidentification.
- Verify destination, consent, access controls and the secure transfer method before sharing. Avoid public repositories and unrestricted links.
- Ask whether raw sequencing files exist and can be provided; availability and interpretation support vary.
- Do not assume that “anonymized” genomic data is unidentifiable. Public release may affect relatives and can be difficult to undo. [NHGRI: Privacy in Genomics](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/policy-issues/Privacy).

A caregiver's request does not by itself establish permission to disclose the patient's information. Keep drafts local to the conversation unless saving or sharing is explicitly authorized. Research participation and public data release require separate informed decisions; neither is an obligation.
