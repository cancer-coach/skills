# Clinical Trial Navigation Guide

Sources checked: **2026-10-15**. Help prepare a shortlist and questions. Only the study team can confirm eligibility, availability, and enrollment; the assistant must not promise access or benefit.

Trials may be relevant at any stage, including initial treatment and supportive care. Discuss them when making care decisions; do not wait automatically until standard treatment is exhausted, or delay needed care to preserve eligibility. [NCI: Clinical Trial Facts](https://www.cancer.gov/research/participate/clinical-trials/facts) (updated 2025-01-03).

## Search and verify

1. Start with the oncology team's trial office and [NCI's trial search](https://www.cancer.gov/research/participate/clinical-trials-search). Search by the documented cancer/subtype, setting, and feasible location.
2. For each candidate, open its official registry record and protocol information. Record the identifier, URL, last update, and date checked.
3. Treat registry status as a lead. A listed site may have no slots or different local status; confirmation requires the study team.
4. Prepare questions using accurate history. Never hide treatments, symptoms, performance limitations, or other eligibility information. Ask the coordinator to explain ambiguous criteria; do not promise exceptions.

## Questions for the trial team

- What is the study testing, and which parts are experimental?
- What alternatives are available, including standard treatment and supportive care?
- What does the phase tell us about this study's purpose and remaining uncertainty?
- Is treatment assigned randomly, blinded, or compared with a control? What would each arm receive?
- What benefits are hoped for, what harms are known, and what is still unknown?
- How do my medicines, prior treatment, organ function and circumstances affect eligibility?
- What extra visits, procedures, travel, costs and caregiver demands are involved?
- Who manages urgent problems locally and outside office hours?
- How does withdrawal work, and what happens after the study or if it does not help?

Phase is not a personal risk score. Read the actual protocol and consent discussion; being listed in a registry is not regulatory approval or proof of efficacy.

Keep clinical details out of public search queries. Draft any inquiry for the person's review and obtain explicit permission before sending or sharing records.
