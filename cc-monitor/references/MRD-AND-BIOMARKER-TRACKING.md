# MRD and Biomarkers: Questions and Records

Sources checked: **2026-10-15**. This guide does not order tests, set a testing cadence, interpret individual results, or recommend changing treatment.

## Separate the purpose of the test

| Purpose | Clarify with the team |
|---------|----------------------|
| Screening | Looking for cancer before symptoms; not a substitute for a known cancer's monitoring plan |
| Tumor profiling | Looking for features relevant to treatment decisions |
| Response or recurrence monitoring | Following disease with an appropriate test in its clinical context |
| Minimal/measurable residual disease (MRD) | Looking for low levels of disease using an assay appropriate to the cancer and setting |

Tumor markers can also change for noncancer reasons and are interpreted with other clinical information. [NCI: Tumor Markers](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis/tumor-markers-fact-sheet).

“Liquid biopsy” describes a sampling approach, not a single interchangeable test. Do not conflate multi-cancer screening, blood-based tumor profiling, and MRD assays. Ask about the exact disease, stage, specimen, assay and intended use. Evidence that a result predicts risk is different from evidence that acting on it improves outcomes.

## Before testing

- What question will this answer in my situation?
- Is it recommended care, an optional test, or research? What evidence supports that use?
- What would positive, negative, or inconclusive results change?
- What could the test miss, and what other assessment remains necessary?
- Who will review results and specify the next contact and follow-up plan?
- What are the financial and emotional burdens, including additional investigations?

## Tracking template

```markdown
Collection date / report date:
Exact test, laboratory and specimen:
Result, units and laboratory reference information copied from report:
Assay/method changes since previous result:
Symptoms or treatment events to report:
Clinician interpretation / date:
Agreed next step / responsible clinician:
```

Keep a result's original wording. Do not label a single abnormal result “noise,” promise that a negative result excludes disease, or infer that rising values mandate treatment. Contact the team using its action plan; do not create a waiting period to establish a trend. A diary or graph never overrides urgent symptoms.
