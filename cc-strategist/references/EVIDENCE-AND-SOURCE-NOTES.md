# Evidence and source notes

Content and links checked **2026-10-15**. This records an editorial evidence check, not clinician review, clinical validation, or a claim that the software improves health outcomes. It does not replace current diagnosis-specific guidance. Recheck official sources before using a treatment approval, trial status, test indication or access rule in an answer; report source dates and uncertainties.

## User-requested experience sources

- [JJ Maxwell, Things to know if you get cancer (2026 Edition)](https://jjmaxwell4.com/writing/things-to-know-when-you-get-cancer/), dated 2026-10-14: a personal account of oral squamous cell carcinoma care. Useful prompts concern scheduling follow-through, second opinions/pathology review, surgery recovery, and keeping visit notes.
- [Hacker News discussion, item 50093568](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50093568), read 2026-10-15: mixed personal experiences and conflicting claims, not verified clinical guidance. Useful themes include trusted supporters, care coordination, care closer to home, and caregiver burden.

**Adaptation:** track whether a test/referral is ordered, booked, completed, received and explained; clarify safe timing for a second opinion; ask about functional recovery and support; verify AI summaries against originals. Do not copy the articles into the skill or treat commenters' reported credentials as verified expertise.

**Rejected as general advice:** letting AI choose a treatment pathway or rule out cancer; hiding prior treatment/plans; universal comprehensive genetic tests; automatic travel based on hospital rankings; assumptions that top centers accept a particular insurance; waiting for hypothetical trials rather than indicated care; unproven dietary/supplement cures. A second opinion agreeing with the first is not inherently a failure. A family's ability to coordinate, travel or research is not a measure of their care or effort.

The earlier pack also drew heavily on Sid Sijbrandij's experience. An exceptional individual course cannot establish that maximal diagnostics or simultaneous experimental treatments caused an outcome, nor predict another person's outcome. This refresh preserves practical agency while removing that causal marketing claim.

## Clinical and practical sources

| Topic | Primary/authoritative source | What it supports and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Communication preferences and a chosen supporter | [NCI: Talking with your doctor](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/coping/adjusting-to-cancer/talk-with-doctors) | Prepare questions, take notes, clarify uncertainty and the person's preferred detail. |
| Treatment conversation | [NCI: Questions to ask about treatment](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/questions) | Goals, alternatives, benefits/harms, timing and practical burden; not AI treatment selection. |
| Care and second opinions | [NCI: Finding cancer care](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/managing-care/finding-cancer-care) | Relevant expertise, records, coordination, access and coverage questions; not a guarantee of better outcomes. |
| Tumor biomarkers | [NCI: Biomarker testing](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/biomarker-testing-cancer-treatment), updated 2021-12-14 | Disease-specific selection and limitations; biomarkers may not yield a useful therapy; VUS are not treatment targets. Page contains older product examples: do not use its catalog as a current approval list. |
| Inherited-risk testing | [NCI: Genetic testing for inherited cancer risk](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/genetics/genetic-testing-fact-sheet) | Distinct from tumor testing; counseling, consent and possible family implications. |
| Trial timing | [NCI: Clinical trial facts](https://www.cancer.gov/research/participate/clinical-trials/facts), updated 2025-01-03 | Trials may apply at any stage; protocol/site-specific review still determines eligibility. |
| Expanded access | [FDA: Expanded access](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/public-health-focus/expanded-access) | Criteria and physician/manufacturer/FDA/IRB roles; authorization does not establish effectiveness or ensure supply. U.S.-specific. |
| Fever during chemotherapy | [CDC: Watch out for fever](https://www.cdc.gov/cancer-preventing-infections/patients/fever.html), dated 2024-11-14 | At least 38°C / 100.4°F means immediate oncology contact and urgent assessment, without a persistence requirement. Lower/team-specific thresholds still apply. |
| Infection and medicine masking fever | [NCI: Infection and neutropenia](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/infection) | Infection symptoms can require contact without fever; check before fever-reducing medicine. Use CDC's consistent 38°C / 100.4°F conversion. |
| Severe acute symptoms | [NHS: Sepsis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sepsis/) | Emergency help for severe breathing problems/confusion; do not diagnose the cause or assume this list covers every cancer emergency. Localize emergency numbers only when location is known. |
| Palliative/supportive care | [NCI: Palliative care](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/advanced-cancer/care-choices/palliative-care-fact-sheet), reviewed 2021-11-01 | Can accompany cancer treatment at any stage; distinguish palliative care from hospice. No individual survival promise. |
| Response assessment | [RECIST working group: iRECIST](https://recist.eortc.org/irecist/) | A technical research framework; clinical stability and clinician judgment matter. Not an instruction to continue treatment or defer evaluation. |
| Monitoring tests | [NCI: Tumor markers](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis/tumor-markers-fact-sheet) | Markers have limitations; interpret alongside other findings. Test availability, prognostic association and improved outcomes from test-guided actions are different claims. |
| Nutrition and supplements | [NCI: Nutrition](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/nutrition), [NCI: Complementary approaches](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam) | Individualized nutrition support and team review of supplements; no fasting/sugar-elimination/supplement cure claims. |

## Use in responses

Cite the source that supports the actual claim. Prefer current official disease-specific guidance, regulator labels and trial registries for time-sensitive specifics. Distinguish approved indication, off-label use, investigation and anecdote. If access is blocked or verification is unavailable, say so and prepare questions rather than inventing an answer. No NCCN content was accessed or reviewed for this update, and no guideline account or agreement was created.

Never include identifying medical details in public searches or external model prompts. Do not label deidentified genomic data anonymous, or assume an AI host is a covered medical provider. A person may share less, decline persistence, or choose a paper record.
